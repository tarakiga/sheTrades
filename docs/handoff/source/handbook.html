01

About this guide

Who it is for, and how to use it.

This handbook covers the SheTrades Digital admin console: the website your team uses to run the programme. It assumes no technical knowledge. Where something genuinely does need a developer, the guide says so plainly rather than leaving you to discover it.

You do not need to read it end to end. Sections 5 to 13 each cover one page of the console and can be read on their own when you need them. Sections 14 and 15 are worth reading once by everyone on the team, because they explain the two rules that govern every change: nothing goes live until it is published, and what you are allowed to do depends on your role.

A note on the screenshots

Every screenshot in this guide is the real console. Learner names, phone numbers and staff email addresses have been replaced with examples, because a document like this gets forwarded. The numbers shown are from the test environment and will not match yours.

02

How the platform works

Two halves, one system.

The first half is the WhatsApp bot. Learners save a phone number, send a message, and are walked through the programme entirely inside WhatsApp: a welcome, a name, a state, a language, then lessons one at a time with a short quiz at the end of each. When they hit a milestone they get airtime. When they finish everything, they get a certificate.

The second half is this console. Almost everything the bot says, offers, or pays out is something you control from here: the lesson text, the quiz questions, the menu wording, the consent notice, the reward amounts, the certificate design. You change it here; the bot picks it up. No developer, no app release, no waiting.

That is the single most important thing to understand about this platform: the words your learners read are your words, held in a place you can edit, with a record of who changed what and a way back to the previous version.

What is not in your hands

Three things sit outside the console and need someone technical, or Meta, to change: the WhatsApp phone number and its verification with Meta; the airtime provider account and its balance; and the underlying software itself. Everything else in this guide is yours.

03

Signing in

Getting into the console, and getting back in when you cannot.

  1. Open the console address your team was given and enter your email and password.
  2. If you have set up two-factor authentication, you will then be asked for the six-digit code from your phone. Enter it and you are in.
  3. You land on the Overview page.

If you cannot get in

Forgotten password
Use Forgot password? on the sign-in screen, or ask any colleague with the Admin role to reset it for you from Settings → Admins. They will give you a temporary password to change once you are in.
Lost the phone with your codes
Ask an Admin colleague to press Reset 2FA against your name in Settings → Admins. That switches two-factor off for your account so you can sign in with your password and set it up again on the new phone.
"Too many attempts"
The console locks an account briefly after several wrong passwords in a row. This is deliberate. Wait a few minutes and try again, or have your password reset.
Do not share logins

Every action in the console is recorded against the person who did it. A shared login destroys that record, and it is the record that protects you when a donor asks who approved a change. Give each person their own account from Settings → Admins.

Finding your way around

The menu down the left, and what each group is for.

The dark sidebar is always there. It is grouped by what you are trying to do, not by how the software is built.

GroupPagesYou come here to…
Overview Overview See how the programme is doing this morning, in one screen.
Engagement Users, Analytics, Content Understand learners, and change what they are taught.
Operations Rewards, Reports, Certificates Run the things learners receive: airtime, exports, credentials.
Configuration Settings, Help Change wording, choices, connections, people and branding. Help opens this guide.

Your own name sits at the bottom of the sidebar. Clicking it opens your profile, where you change your password and set up two-factor authentication.

Guided tours

Some pages have a Take a tour button in the top right. It walks you through that screen in about a minute. They are worth doing once each, and you can skip out at any point.

Help is always one click away

Help, at the bottom of the sidebar, opens this guide in a new tab so you can read it beside the screen you are stuck on. It is part of the console rather than a file someone emailed you, so it is updated whenever the platform is.

05

Overview

The morning screen. Start here, then go looking.

The four figures

Registered Learners
How many people have started a conversation and given their name. This is your reach.
Module Completion
How far registered learners are getting through the material. Low completion with high registration means people are arriving but not staying.
Quiz Pass Rate
Of the quizzes attempted, how many were passed. Read this next to completion, not on its own. A high pass rate with low completion usually means the few who persist are doing fine and the rest are dropping out before the quiz.
Rewards Automation
How much of the airtime that should have gone out actually did. Anything below 100% means some payments failed and need looking at on the Rewards page.

Operational Review

The table below the figures turns them into a verdict. Each row carries a status pill (Healthy, Watch or Idle), so you can scan for the one that needs you rather than interpreting four percentages yourself.

The support panels

  • Recent Reward Activity: the last airtime payments, newest first.
  • Users requesting help: learners who tapped the help option during a lesson check-in. These are people who asked for a human. They are the highest-value list on this page.
  • At-Risk Learners: registered but barely progressing.
  • Module Funnel Snapshot: how many made it from registered, to started, to completed, to quiz passed.
  • Upcoming Milestones: learners about to cross a reward threshold, so you can check the airtime balance before it matters.

Export Summary in the top right downloads what is on this page as a spreadsheet, which is usually the quickest way to answer "how are we doing?" in an email.

06

Users

Every learner, and what to do about the ones who are stuck.

The table lists every learner with their location, language, how far through they are, and whether they are active. Flagged only narrows it to the people someone has already marked for follow-up. Export Users downloads the list.

The three icons on each row

Eye: open her details
Opens the panel in Fig 6.2 without leaving the list.
Speech bubble: message on WhatsApp
Opens WhatsApp with her number ready, so you can write to her personally. This is a human conversation from your own phone or desktop WhatsApp, not an automated message.
Flag: mark for follow-up
Adds her to the flagged list so your team knows she needs attention. Add a note saying why.

The panel shows her progress, her quiz attempts, any rewards she has received, and, once she has earned one, a link to her certificate. State under Session is the step of the conversation she is currently on; if it says she is waiting to answer something, she is, and a nudge on WhatsApp will usually restart her.

07

Analytics

Where learners fall out, and how to tell teaching problems from testing problems.

Reading the funnel

The funnel counts people at each stage: registered, started a module, completed it, attempted the quiz, passed. The useful information is in the gaps between the bars, not the bars themselves.

The gap you seeWhat it usually meansWhere to act
Registered → Started drops sharply Onboarding is too long, or the first lesson is not compelling Content: the welcome and first module
Started → Completed drops sharply Lessons are too long, or arriving at the wrong pace Content: lesson length and structure
Completed → Quiz attempted drops The quiz prompt is unclear or feels like a test Content: the wording that introduces the quiz
Quiz attempted → Passed drops Questions are harder than the lesson prepared people for Content: the questions themselves

Overall shows the totals; Delta shows the change since the last snapshot, which is what tells you whether something you changed last month worked. Download CSV gives you the underlying figures for a report.

Why the numbers might not move instantly

Analytics is calculated from the events the bot records, and the page takes a snapshot rather than streaming live. Reload the page to pull a fresh one.

Content

Every word the bot says. The most powerful page in the console.

Things you will change most often

Not everything lives on this page. Start here, find the row for what you want to change, and go where it sends you.

What you want to changeWhere it livesSearch for
The privacy notice the bot shows
Before anything is collected, after she picks a language.		 Content privacy
The full privacy policy
The web page the notice links to.		 Settings → Legal Privacy policy
FAQ questions and answers
The entries themselves, not the wording around them.		 Settings → Options FAQs
The resources menu
Links and guidance offered to learners.		 Settings → Options Resources
Lesson text and quiz questions Content The lesson title
What the bot says around all of it
Welcome, menus, buttons, congratulations, error messages.		 Content Filter to Chatbot Prompts
Reward amounts and when they are paid Settings → Rewards No search needed

The pattern worth learning: Content holds words the bot says. Settings → Options holds lists it offers, where each entry can be switched on or off. FAQs and resources appear in both, and the difference matters: the wording around them is in Content, the entries themselves are in Options.

Resources needs your content before it is useful

Resources already appears on the bot's main menu, and learners can tap it today. It ships with two sample entries, both switched off, so nobody is shown placeholder text; what they get instead is a short message saying resources are not available yet.

To turn it on: Settings → Options → Resources, replace the two samples with your own, switch each one on, and publish. It starts working the moment one enabled entry is live. Nothing else needs changing, and there is no switch to flip elsewhere.

What lives here

Two kinds of thing, in one table:

  • Lessons: the teaching material, each with its text, an optional audio version, and the quiz at the end.
  • Bot messages: everything else the bot says: the welcome, the main menu, the prompt that asks for a name, the privacy notice, the congratulations at the end of a module, and the wording that frames the FAQ and resource menus.

The chips above the table (All, Draft, Live, Trash) filter it. Draft is the one to watch: anything sitting there is a change somebody started and has not published, so learners are not seeing it yet.

Changing something that already exists

EditorAdmin can edit · only Admin can publish
  1. Find the item. Search by its title, or by the small grey name beneath it.
  2. Click the pencil to open it.
  3. Make your change. The editor formats text the way WhatsApp does, with *bold* and _italics_, and shows you a preview.
  4. Save. This creates a draft. Nothing has changed for learners yet.
  5. Publish when you are happy. The bot uses the new wording from the next message it sends.

Creating something new

  1. Info: choose a category and give it a short name. The console assembles the internal reference itself so it stays consistent.
  2. Content: write the text, and add quiz questions if it is a lesson.
  3. Preview: see it as a learner will, then save it as a draft.

There is a Raw JSON Editor tab beside the wizard. Ignore it; it exists for developers, and the wizard can do everything you need.

Quiz questions

Each lesson can carry two kinds of question, and the difference matters:

Knowledge question
Has a correct answer. Getting it right counts towards the pass rate.
Check-in question
Every answer is accepted. One option can be marked as a request for help. A learner who picks it is flagged for follow-up and appears on the Overview page. Use this to find people who are struggling but would never say so directly.
WhatsApp limits your wording

WhatsApp allows only 24 characters on a button and 24 on a menu row. The editor shows a meter and will not let you exceed it. This is not the console being fussy: longer text is silently cut off by WhatsApp itself, mid-word, on the learner's phone.

Categories and the bin

Manage Categories groups items so the table stays navigable as it grows. Deleting an item moves it to Trash, where it can be restored; Empty Trash is the only permanent step, and it asks first.

Other languages

English is live. Pidgin and Igbo appear to learners as coming soon. When you are ready to add them, the Translations area (Settings → Integration → Translations) drafts a translation of every piece of content for a person to review and approve before anything is published. Machine translation is a starting point here, never the final word.

09

Rewards

The airtime learners earn, and what to do when a payment fails.

Learners earn airtime automatically when they cross a milestone. This page is where you watch that happening and step in when it does not.

The three states

Issued
Sent and confirmed by the airtime provider. Nothing to do.
Pending
Queued, not yet confirmed. Normal for a few minutes. Pending for hours means the provider is not responding. Check Settings → Integration → Payouts.
Failed
Rejected. Almost always an empty provider wallet or a mistyped phone number. The row gives the reason.

Fixing a failure

  1. Filter to Failed and read the reason on the row.
  2. If the wallet is empty, top it up with the airtime provider, then reissue.
  3. If the number is wrong, correct it on the learner's record first, or the reissue fails the same way.
  4. Use Issue Manual Reward to send it by hand once the cause is fixed.
Manual rewards spend real money

Issue Manual Reward sends airtime immediately and cannot be recalled. Check the amount and the number before confirming. Every manual reward is recorded against your name.

The amounts and the milestones themselves are not set here. They live in Settings → Rewards, covered in section 12.

10

Reports

Exports for donors and stakeholders, on demand or on a schedule.

A report right now

Press Generate Report, choose a preset, and the export appears in Export History to download when it is ready. The presets are shaped for different audiences: Donor leads with impact and completion, Ops with operational detail, Finance with reward totals.

A report every week, automatically

EditorAdmin
  1. Create Schedule under Scheduled Jobs.
  2. Pick the preset and how often it should run.
  3. Choose who receives it. Your admin team appears automatically; external stakeholders come from a recipient list you maintain in Settings → Options; or type a one-off address.
  4. Save. Each schedule card then shows its next run and how the last one went.

Pause stops a schedule without deleting it, and resuming does not send the runs it missed. Run Now sends immediately without disturbing the schedule, which is useful when a donor asks between reporting dates.

Keep the recipient list current

These emails contain learner data. Maintaining external recipients in the shared list rather than typing addresses each time means you have one place to look when someone leaves a partner organisation, and removing them there removes them from every schedule at once.

11

Certificates

What a learner receives for finishing, and how you design it.

When a learner completes every module, the bot congratulates her, confirms the name to print, and sends the certificate as an image in the chat along with a link anyone can open to verify it. She can ask for it again at any time from the bot's main menu.

Switching issuing on and off

Admin only

Turning issuing on is confirmed, because the next learner to finish receives a permanent, publicly shareable credential and a WhatsApp message cannot be recalled. Turning it off stops new certificates; the ones already issued stay valid and their links keep working.

Managing certificates already issued

Edit name
Corrects a name a learner typed badly. The verification link does not change, so a link she has already shared keeps working.
Resend
Sends the same certificate again. Use it when a message never arrived.
Revoke
Marks it as no longer valid, with a reason you record. Anyone opening its page is told plainly. This can be undone.

Designing the certificate

EditorAdmin can design · only Admin can publish

Certificates → Edit the design opens a screen with two panels, and the difference between them is the single thing to understand here:

  • The Layout panel on the left shows dashed boxes over the artwork. Each box is a handle: it marks where a field is anchored and how much width it may use. Drag it, or select it and use the arrow keys (hold Shift for bigger steps).
  • The Preview panel on the right is the certificate itself, drawn by the same machinery that draws a real one. It refreshes a second or two after you finish a change.
Approve the preview, never the canvas

The boxes are instruments for positioning; the preview is the artefact. They are not the same picture, and only the preview is what a learner receives. Before publishing, use the Preview for selector to check the design against a long name and an all-capitals name. That is how a layout that looked fine ends up with somebody's own name running off her certificate.

Uploading artwork

PNG, JPEG or SVG, up to 5 MB. A background must be at least 800 × 600.

Artwork is never replaced

Upload a new version of a logo and it gets a new name, and the box suggests a free one for you. This is deliberate. Every certificate already issued still points at the artwork it was issued with, so overwriting a picture would quietly redraw credentials that are already in learners' hands, months later.

Saving, publishing, rolling back

Save draft stores your work and changes nothing for learners. Publish makes it the design the next certificate is issued with. Write a note saying what changed, because it is kept in the history. History lists every published version with a way back to it. Rolling back does not alter certificates already issued: each one keeps a frozen copy of the design it was granted under, which is why an old verification link still shows what that learner was actually given.

12

Settings

Six areas. Choices, legal text, connections, reward rules, people, and branding.

Options: the lists people choose from

EditorAdmin

Every list of choices in the system lives here: the states a learner can pick during onboarding, the languages offered, the FAQ topics, the resources menu, report recipients, report cadences. Add an entry, reorder them, or switch one off without deleting it. If a state should no longer be offered, disable it here and it disappears from the bot, and the learners who already chose it keep their record.

Legal: consent and policy text

EditorAdmin

The consent notice, the privacy policy, the data statement. Edited with a proper text editor, and every version is kept. If a learner or a regulator asks what she agreed to in March, the history is the answer.

Integration: connections to the outside world

Admin only
WhatsApp
The phone line the bot speaks through. Shows whether it is live and healthy.
Notification
The email account used for admin invitations and scheduled reports. If reports stop arriving, check here first.
Payouts
The airtime provider. Failed rewards usually trace back to this tab.
Translations
The service that drafts translations for a human to review.

Each has a test connection action. Use it after any change rather than waiting to find out from a learner.

The WhatsApp sandbox

This is the most useful tool in the console and the least obvious. It is a simulated WhatsApp conversation running against your live wording, so you can walk the whole learner journey (welcome, name, state, language, a lesson, a quiz) without touching a real phone or a real learner. Use it every time you change something a learner reads. Reset Session State starts the conversation over from the beginning.

Rewards: how much, and when

Admin only

This sets what learners earn and at which milestones. Edit Rule opens the form; the change becomes a draft; Publish Live makes it real. Restore Previous puts the last version back.

This is a budget decision

Changing an amount changes what every future learner receives. Multiply the new amount by the learners you expect and check it against the airtime wallet before publishing.

Admins: your team

Admin only

Add Admin creates an account with a temporary password the person changes on first sign-in. Change someone's role from the dropdown on their row.

  • Suspend blocks access while keeping the account and its history. This is what you want when someone goes on leave or a contract pauses.
  • Delete removes the account permanently.
  • Reset password and Reset 2FA are how you rescue a colleague who is locked out.
When someone leaves

Suspend the same day. Their past actions stay in the record, which is exactly what you want: you are removing access, not erasing history.

Branding: making it yours

Admin only

The organisation name and the three brand colours used across the console, the bot's welcome message, and outgoing emails. The preview updates as you change them. This is what makes the platform reusable for another programme without rebuilding it.

13

Your account

Your password, and the one security step worth doing today.

Change your display name, your password, and set up two-factor authentication.

Two-factor authentication

With it on, signing in needs your password and a six-digit code from your phone, so a stolen password is not enough on its own. Setting it up takes about two minutes:

  1. Press Set up two-factor authentication.
  2. Scan the square code with an authenticator app. Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator and Authy all work.
  3. Type the six digits it shows to confirm.
  4. Save the recovery codes somewhere safe and offline. They are how you get in if you lose the phone.
Strongly recommended for anyone who can approve payouts

An Admin account can send real money and publish text to thousands of learners. Two-factor authentication is the difference between a stolen password being an inconvenience and being an incident.

14

How changes go live

The one rule that applies everywhere in the console.

Almost nothing you edit takes effect the moment you save it. Every change moves through the same three states, and knowing them will save you more confusion than anything else in this guide.

StateWho sees itHow it moves on
Draft Only your team, inside the console An Admin publishes it
Live Learners, from the next message the bot sends It is replaced when something newer is published
Previous versions Nobody, but they are kept Restore one to make it live again

Every version records who made it, when, and the note they wrote. That record is what lets you answer "who changed the consent text and when" months later, so write a real note, not "update".

Undoing something

  1. Open the item and find its history or version list.
  2. Choose the version that was correct.
  3. Restore it. It is published as a new version rather than erasing what happened, so the record stays honest.
If a change does not seem to have taken

Check the three usual causes in order: it is still a draft; you are looking at a page that has not been reloaded; or you are testing on a phone whose conversation is mid-way through a step and has not reached the message you changed. The sandbox (Settings → Integration) settles it in under a minute.

Who can do what

Three roles, deliberately different.

Action Viewer Editor Admin
See learners, analytics and contentYesYesYes

The split between Editor and Admin is the important one: anyone trusted with the programme can write, but only an Admin decides what reaches learners. That is why a programme officer can draft a better lesson on a Tuesday without being able to change the reward budget by accident.

A sensible starting shape

Two Admins, never one, or a holiday becomes an outage. Editors for everyone who writes content or handles learners. Viewer for M&E staff and partner observers who need the numbers but should not change anything.

16

Your operating rhythm

A suggested routine, so nothing waits for someone to notice it.

Every day (five minutes)

  • Overview: is anything showing Watch?
  • Users requesting help: anyone new? Reply to them personally.
  • Rewards: any Failed payments?

Every week

  • Check the airtime wallet against Upcoming Milestones. Running out is the most common preventable failure on this platform.
  • Look at flagged learners and clear the list.
  • Skim the funnel on Analytics for a stage that has got worse.

Every month

  • Review the drop-off with the biggest gap and change the content behind it.
  • Check Settings → Admins: does everyone listed still work here?
  • Confirm the scheduled reports actually arrived.
  • Send a certificate verification link to yourself and open it, to confirm the public page is still working.

If something looks wrong

The common ones, and what to check first.

What you seeMost likely causeWhat to do
Learners are not getting replies The WhatsApp connection has dropped Settings → Integration → WhatsApp, test the connection
A wording change has not appeared It is still a draft Publish it, then check in the sandbox
Rewards all failing at once Empty airtime wallet Top up with the provider, then reissue the failed ones
Scheduled report did not arrive Email account or a wrong recipient address Settings → Integration → Notification; check the recipient list
No certificates being issued Issuing is switched off Certificates → the Issuing card
A learner's name is wrong on her certificate She typed it that way Certificates → Edit name; her link keeps working
The console will not let you publish You have the Editor role Ask an Admin colleague to publish
Numbers look stale The page is showing an older snapshot Reload the page

When to call for technical help

Everything above is yours to fix. Escalate when the whole console will not load, when a connection test keeps failing after the credentials have been re-entered, when Meta changes something about the WhatsApp number, or when you see the same error after trying the steps here twice. Say what you did, what you expected, and what happened instead. That alone usually halves the time to a fix.

19

Handover checklist

Work through this once, together, before the programme is yours to run.

Access

  • Every person who needs the console has their own account, with the right role.
  • At least two people hold the Admin role.
  • Two-factor authentication is on for every Admin, and the recovery codes are stored somewhere safe and offline.
  • Any accounts created during the build that your team does not recognise are suspended.

Content

  • Someone on your team has edited a lesson, published it, and seen the change in the sandbox.
  • The consent and privacy wording has been read and approved by whoever is accountable for it.
  • The FAQ and resources lists are populated with real answers.

Money

  • The reward amounts and milestones are the ones you intend to fund.
  • The airtime wallet is topped up, and someone owns keeping it that way.
  • You have watched one reward go out successfully.

Certificates

  • The design has been signed off from the preview, checked against a long name.
  • Issuing is switched on when, and only when, you are ready.
  • One learner has been through the whole programme and received hers, and you have opened her verification link yourself.

Reporting

  • The schedules donors expect are set up, with the right recipients.
  • One scheduled report has actually arrived in an inbox.
